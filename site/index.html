About me

My central question is how sustainability becomes part of normal software engineering work, rather than remaining an organisational aspiration or an isolated technical activity.

Through empirical research with financial-services practitioners, I explore developer perspectives, the feasibility of sustainability tools, organisational adoption conditions and the practical difficulties of introducing change in Agile teams. I also collaborate on research concerning LLM-supported Agile coaching, participatory AI fairness, and human control in generative-AI creativity tools.

My work draws on industry software engineering, university teaching, educational software design and research mentorship. I'm interested in research that connects sound engineering methods to the people and organisations that use them.

Research focus

Research grounded in the reality of software teams - including the environmental, technical, economic, social and individual dimensions of sustainability.

Sustainability in software engineering teams

Exploring how developers, team leads and managers understand sustainability and how their priorities and constraints affect everyday decisions and organisational practice.

Feasibility and adoption of sustainability tools

Investigating deployment, data-access demands, outputs, workflow fit, reviewability and organisational responsibility as practical conditions for introducing sustainability-oriented tools.

Human-centred AI and AI for software engineering

Contributing to participatory AI fairness, AI-enhanced educational tools, LLM-assisted Agile coaching and human control in generative-AI creation workflows.

Industry collaboration

My Barclays-funded doctoral research is carried out in collaboration with software practitioners in the financial-services sector. It includes industrial case studies and longitudinal work on sustainability-oriented software engineering.
