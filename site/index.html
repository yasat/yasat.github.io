About me

I study how sustainability becomes part of everyday software engineering practices and organisational culture.

I am a PhD researcher in the School of Computing Science at the University of Glasgow. My work considers environmental, technical, economic, social and individual sustainability. Through empirical and longitudinal studies with financial-services practitioners, I investigate developer perspectives, organisational priorities, the adoption of sustainability tools and improvements to Agile processes.

My broader research interests include human-centred AI and AI for Software Engineering (AI4SE), with contributions to participatory AI fairness, LLM-supported Agile coaching and human agency in generative AI tools.

Alongside research, I teach software engineering, develop AI-supported learning tools, supervise MSc students, mentor postgraduate researchers and contribute to university software projects. My goal is to develop sustainable and fair methods and technologies that reflect the needs of the people and organisations affected by them.

Research focus

My PhD focuses on sustainable software engineering in industry, alongside collaborative work in human-centred AI and AI for software engineering.

Sustainability mindsets, practice and culture

Doctoral research | Five dimensions of sustainability

I investigate how environmental, technical, economic, social and individual sustainability are understood and acted upon by software engineers and management in financial-services organisations.

Empirical studies explore practitioner priorities, organisational constraints and the everyday decisions that influence whether sustainability becomes embedded in software engineering practice.

Feasibility and adoption of sustainability tools

Mixed-methods industrial research

My research examines the practical feasibility of sustainability tools and techniques through industry workshops and surveys. It identifies issues around tool integration, data access, output usefulness and organisational culture.

Follow-on research studies how actionability, reviewability, workflow integration and organisational ownership influence whether technically feasible tools and practices are adopted.

Sustainability-oriented Agile process improvement

Longitudinal industrial action research

I study changes to Agile software processes over time, including sustainability-oriented improvements discussed and followed up in team retrospectives.

This work considers the influence of workloads, technical constraints and organisational dependencies on improvement decisions, and why teams must reassess feasibility and unintended effects as circumstances change.

Human-centred AI and AI for software engineering

Collaborative research

I contribute to work on how lay stakeholders can define their own fairness criteria for AI assessments and how those criteria might inform bias mitigation.

I also research LLM-supported Agile coaching, generative AI in sustainability requirements elicitation, and human agency in multimodal AI video authoring.

Evaluating the Usefulness of Large Language Models in Learning Agile Coaching

2026
Ghanta, S., Obode, E., Luo, L., Hall, C., Storer, T., Gregory, P. & Çalıklı, G. | CSEE&T 2026Accepted for publication | First author

Evaluated two LLMs using four realistic Agile-coaching scenarios and expert assessment.

The models produced practical, context-sensitive coaching plans but often lacked evaluation measures, alternative explanations and risk awareness without follow-up prompts, reinforcing the importance of structured questioning and human judgement.

Submitted manuscripts and preprints

From Feasibility to Adoption: Perceptions of Sustainable Software Engineering Tools and Practices

2026
Ghanta, S., Gregory, P. & Çalıklı, G. | Submitted to ICSE 2027, Software Engineering in Practice (SEIP)Submitted | First author

Four focus groups with 17 financial-services practitioners investigated seven perceived conditions for adopting sustainability tools and practices, including actionability, reviewability, workflow integration and organisational ownership.

The work identifies three gaps: measurement without diagnosis, automated changes without reviewable trust, and planning data without governance.

When Sustainability Meets Practice: Challenges of Sustainability-Oriented Software Process Improvement in Industry

2026
Ghanta, S., Gregory, P., Çalıklı, G. & Salimy, A. | Submitted to ICSE 2027 SEIPSubmitted | First author

Reports 18 months of industry action research, including senior-management planning, a four-month pilot and a nine-month main study with Agile teams.

Retrospectives, observations and follow-ups show how workload, technical constraints and organisational dependencies influence sustainability-related changes and the need to reassess adverse effects and feasibility.

Use of Generative AI for Sustainable Software Requirements Elicitation: An Exploratory Comparison of Students and Industry Practitioners

2026
Ghanta, S., Salimy, A., Bathala, V. T., Gross, J., Adil, M., Gregory, P., Çalıklı, G. & Ouhbi, S. | Submitted to Automated Software Engineering journalSubmitted | First author

Four focus groups with eight MSc students and eight industry practitioners compared GenAI-assisted with conventional sustainability requirements elicitation.

GenAI supported brainstorming and new perspectives, while non-GenAI groups produced more requirements and engaged in more clarification, negotiation and critical discussion. Human judgement and structured refinement remain important.

What Changes When the Story Changes? Governing Revision in Multimodal AI Video Authoring

2026
Luo, L.* & Ghanta, S.* | Submitted to ACM CHISubmitted | Co-first author (equal contribution)

Co-developed AnimWeave, a dependency-aware AI video-authoring prototype, with a formative workshop involving six creators and a 12-participant user study.

The manuscript introduces revision governance to understand creators’ choices about revising, preserving or reinterpreting AI-generated artefacts and delegating control to AI.

Empowering Affected Individuals to Shape AI Fairness Assessments: Processes, Criteria, and Tools

2026
Luo, L., Ghanta, S., Nakao, Y., Chollet, M. & Stumpf, S. | arXiv:2602.06984; submitted to ACM TOCHIPreprint available | TOCHI manuscript submitted

Investigates the involvement of individuals affected by AI decisions without formal AI expertise in fairness assessment and bias mitigation.

An interactive prototype supported 18 participants in developing 47 custom fairness criteria. Three strategies for selecting and integrating stakeholder-defined criteria into constraint-based bias mitigation were evaluated.
